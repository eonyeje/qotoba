Workbench prototype guide

A clickable HTML prototype of the Qotoba Workbench: sixteen screens and ten page templates, three layouts, light and dark, filled with one consistent snapshot of the programme at 05:12 on Friday 6 November 2026. Revised the same day: every screen now leads with the one thing to decide, and everything else waits one tap away. Revised again: features, bugs and performance problems are now issues kept apart from tasks, each with its own report page. Round 1 changes content only (wording, missing facts, spacing and samples); the flow, panes, tabs and navigation are as approved. Round 2 makes the inbox the only place you act: reports, task pages, sessions and Rulings show decisions as information, and every decision reaches you as a card that cites its task and step. Use this page to review the prototype against the brief.

HTML, CSS and JavaScript onlyno build, no networkopens from the file system

Open it

Open index.html straight from the file system or any static server. The header holds the layout switcher (Phone 390 px, Rider 460 px, Desktop 1200 px and up), which constrains the shell to that width so all three can be reviewed in one browser window. Press ? or the grid button for the Screens drawer: every screen, sample card, item, template, overlay and empty state by name, plus layout and theme switches. Theme follows your system; the drawer and Settings can force light or dark.

Design approach

The Workbench exists to cut what reaches you, so each screen shows the least that lets you decide and lets you go deeper one section at a time.

  • One decision first. A card opens on its kind, one sentence, the choices and the buttons. Nothing else is open.
  • Drill rows for the rest. Every other section is a single line: a label, a one-line summary, a chevron. Open the ones you care about. A small amber dot marks a row that deserves a look, for example “2 assumptions to review”.
  • “Worth a glance” on packets. Under “All 5 checks passed” sits the short list of exceptions. Each one opens its own section.
  • Tabs for long pages. The task page has seven tabs: Overview, About, Plan, Changes, Issues, Decisions, Activity. An issue report has five: Report, Plan, Changes, Decisions, Activity. Old tab links (questions, runs and so on) still work and open the matching section.
  • Your own words on every card. Question, Approve, Plan, Stuck and Info cards each have a reply box beside the default buttons. Type a message and press Send or Enter; it goes to the session that raised the card. Approve's “Ask for changes” opens the same box, with no picker.
  • Quiet chips. A chip shows its icon and title only. IDs, dates and back-links are inside the popover under “Where it came from”. Breadcrumbs are plain links.
  • Short lists. An inbox card has a kind, a headline and one line of context, with no buttons. “For you to know” is folded into one line. In the tree each row is a title and one state word, and filters sit behind one Filter button.
  • Space. Wider margins, a reading column of about 780 px on desktop, a one-row header, and the project filter as a single menu.

Content rules

Round 1 left every route, pane, tab bar, breadcrumb and expansion as approved and changed only what the screens say and how much room it gets. These are the rules the content now follows.

  • Why a card reached you. Every pushed card has one line under its headline naming the rule that pushed it, in plain words: “Blocking question on step 3”, “All checks passed, ready to merge”, “Failed three times on the same step”, “Plan needs your approval”. It is the only place the mechanism is explained.
  • What answering unblocks. Every inbox row has its own line: “Unblocks Run page with output · about 2 h 10 min of session time”.
  • Exceptions, not totals. Glance lines and section summaries name what is unusual (“1 check not passing”, “1 claim without a run”, “3 files new to you”). When nothing is, they say “Nothing unusual” in muted text.
  • Required actions in the inbox only. Anything you must do sits on a card. The plan card's “What I need from you” holds its settle pass (“3 defaults to confirm” with Accept all). Reports, task pages and sessions have no such strip; see One place to act.
  • Options you can weigh. On a Question Card, and on a report's Decision lines, the recommended option comes first and says so; every option has a second line with its reason and, on the same line, its own cost to change.
  • Assumptions to review, in full. On the packet card the ones still to review are listed with room around them; the reviewed ones collapse into one muted line, “2 reviewed”. The button says how many it covers: “All 2 fine”. Elsewhere the same list is read only.
  • Numbers with units and a reference. “7 h 23 min left of 11 h 25 min forecast”, “0.6 s, was 4.8 s”, “factor 1.3”, “waiting 1 h 14 min”, “cited in 3 places”. No bare numbers.
  • Nothing cut off. Tabs, breadcrumb segments, section titles and chip titles wrap onto a second line instead of ending in “…”. Shortened summaries end at a whole word.
  • Empty sections are not drawn. A section with nothing in it does not appear as an empty row.
  • Where a thing came from, first. A chip's popover opens with its origin line in normal text (“Answered on a question card, 5 Nov”), then the thing itself.
  • Author labels you can read. You, Claude and machine labels on report sections are larger, keep their colours, and are readable at phone width.
  • Room to breathe. Section rows, option rows, glance lines and list rows have more vertical space, and lines that were packed together now sit apart. Pages got longer; nothing got denser.
  • One name for the last group. “About this item” on every kind of page, issues included.

One place to act

Round 2 removes every decision that lived outside the inbox and changes the data so that none can arise again. Round 0's panes, tabs, breadcrumbs and folds are unchanged, and round 1's spacing still holds.

  • Reports are information. No report has a “What I need from you” strip, and nothing on a report confirms, chooses, sets or ticks. Severity, dependencies, the recommended fix and a PER target show as Claude's proposal with the Claude author label. You can change them through Edit at the top of the report, the same kind of control as Rename on a task: nothing waits on it.
  • When implementing. Every report carries this section, written by Claude and kept current. Each line is a Decision, Proof, Dependency or Precondition and shows one of six statuses: No task has taken this on, Scheduled, Asked, Settled, Proved or Dropped. Scheduled, Asked, Settled and Proved lines carry a chip to the task, card, ruling or run that moved them. It is the only source a task's decisions are drawn from.
  • Report tabs. Plan and Changes show the task that took the report on, with chips to it and its plan card, or say no task has. Decisions shows the When implementing lines by kind, read only. A task's own Decisions tab opens with Decided: each decision made for it, how (settle pass or Question Card), when, and chips to the card and the report it came from.
  • Lifecycle. “About this item” opens with Reported → Taken on → In progress → Done → Verified. Task events move it; you never do. Under the headline the report says “Taken on by” with the task chips, or “No task yet”.
  • How a report gets a task. Add item in the tree has a new field, Implements, with a tick per report. A task may implement several reports, and a report may be split across tasks. The task page shows “Implements” with the report chips.
  • How a decision reaches you. The task's plan card draws the report's lines: a low-cost Decision and a Dependency become settle-pass defaults that cite the report (“Which fix? from BUG-2”), a high-cost Decision becomes a Question Card when the session reaches its step, and a Proof becomes a plan step. Approving the plan settles the defaults; the run proves the line.
  • Assumptions and found items. Reviewed and sorted only on the packet card (“All 2 fine”, Flip, and Task · Later · Drop per found item). Task Decisions tabs and session pages show the same lists read only, with each item's state and a chip to the packet card. Task on a found item opens Add item, prefilled; the item enters the tree only when you add it.
  • Rulings proposed from chat. One Record card in the inbox, “4 rulings proposed from chat”, with the tick boxes and Record ticked. Rulings › Proposed from chat shows them read only with a chip to the card.
  • Questions not asked. There is no deferred state outside the inbox. FTR-2's two class-construct questions are now Decision lines with “No task has taken this on”. Snoozed cards stay in the inbox's own deferred band.
  • The inbox. Still three kinds: Question, Action (Approve, Plan, Stuck and the new Record card) and Info. Every row carries tags for its task, its step and where it came from; on the card the same line is chips. “Why you are seeing this” names the task and step. Only Inbox shows a count in the navigation; Now and Issues never show a dot.

Every control, classed

A script walks every route in the Screens drawer plus every card, report, task and session (140 routes) in each layout, opens every fold and the sheets and menus listed, and classes each button, tick box, menu item and tappable row as one of three. Inbox action: answers a card; allowed only on the card. Owner structure: something you start on your own structure with nothing waiting on it. Navigation: opens a pane, a popover, a filter or a search. It found no unclassed control, no inbox action outside the inbox, no strip or confirm, choose, set, tick or All fine control on a report, a When implementing section on every report with every line in one of the six statuses, origin on every card, and no nav dot except Inbox. Issues and reports

Tasks and issues are two separate lists. A task is part of a project's work breakdown, the tree under Work. An issue is a feature, bug or performance problem (FTR, BUG, PER) that comes up while a task is being built. It is attached to that task, or to a project when no task fits, but it is its own record with its own report, state, time and dependencies.

  • Where issues show. The Issues screen lists every issue grouped by the task it hangs off, with a kind filter and, on each row, the task that has taken it on or “no task yet”. Work has a Tasks | Issues switch. Each task has an Issues tab, and a tree row carries a small issue count. Desktop and Rider also show Issues in the navigation; on phone, use the switch on Work.
  • Dependencies. An issue can need another issue or a task, and a task can need an issue. Each edge says why, whether it is hard or soft, and who declared it. Blocks are worked out from Needs, never typed. An edge Claude proposed stays a proposal until a plan confirms it in its settle pass; nothing waits on you. Report links carry no minutes: the “unblocks” weight belongs to cards.
  • The report. It opens like a task page: kind and state, the title, one sentence (your goal or the symptom), the tabs, one headline such as “Fix 2 settled” or “0.6 s”, and “Taken on by” with task chips or “No task yet”. Every section below is a closed one-line row in a fixed order, marked you, Claude or machine; open only what you want, and the page remembers per kind. Inside a section, code, stacks, attachments and a fix's approach sit behind a second fold. Lifecycle, severity, dependencies, time and origin are closed rows under “About this item”.
  • When implementing. The first section on every report: what a task will have to settle, each line with its status and the chip that moved it. See One place to act.

What is clickable

  • Every chip opens a popover (an anchored card on desktop, a bottom sheet on phone and Rider) with the thing itself, its origin, back-links, Open and Copy link. Open always lands on the right screen.
  • Navigation: list to detail, tab to tab, breadcrumb chips, Back (hash history), the bottom bar on phone, the icon rail in Rider, the left navigation on desktop, the “⋯ More” menu on phone.
  • Review controls: layout switcher, project filter (applies everywhere), inbox sort and deferred toggle, Work filters and Outline/Columns, Files scope, rulings search.
  • Buttons that would change data raise a toast and change nothing, for example “Instruction sent to the Host on laptop for session c21e”. Folds, plan Change mode, settle choices, flips and tick boxes respond on screen.
  • Replies: every card's reply box takes your own text; Send or Enter raises a toast naming the session and machine that would receive it.
  • Issue reports: Edit opens rename, severity, recommended fix, target and dependencies as sheets that raise a toast. Nothing on a report needs you.
  • Overlays: Talk (the chat brief, shown disabled), Add item (with Implements, and prefilled from a found item), Move, Rename, report Edit, Message, Reassign, Make a ruling, beyond-plan justification.

Counts that must agree

Counts are worked out from the data, not typed into screens: the header meter, the Inbox badge, the Needs you band and Settings all count the same 7 cards; deferred cards are left out. Issue counts on tree rows, the Issues tab and the Issues screen come from the same attach field, and an issue's “taken on by” comes from the tasks' Implements field. An item's “questions n/2” is counted from its open questions, so WB-2.2 shows 2/2 on its cards, its summary and its Questions tab. Comment counts (5 open, 3 handled, 1 stale) are the same in Files, the Comments tab and WB-2's summary. Back-link totals (“cited by”) are worked out by scanning every record for chips.

Limits

  • Fonts fall back to Segoe UI and Consolas because the prototype makes no network calls; the documentation pages load the web fonts.
  • Desktop mode keeps the shell at least 1200 px wide, so a narrower browser scrolls sideways in that mode only.
  • Drag handles in plan Change mode are visual; reordering does not move steps.
  • Layout, theme and fold choices are remembered in this browser only. Report edits raise a toast and change nothing.
